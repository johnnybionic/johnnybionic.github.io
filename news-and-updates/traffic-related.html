Low Traffic Neighbourhood (LTN) Mitigations for Charlton

We are currently waiting to hear about mitigations for increased levels of traffic and air pollution in Charlton. This was recommended at the call-in meeting in November 2025 after the council’s report showed that traffic had been displaced to Charlton (particularly Eastcombe Avenue, Victoria Way and Marlborough Lane) by the West & East Greenwich Management Scheme / LTNs.

The West & East Greenwich LTNs were made permanent in March 2026 and a Labour pre-election letter to Charlton residents promised: “a meaningful mitigation measures for Charlton will be introduced as soon as possible” and a public consultation later this year. Potential options mentioned were:

  • A range of options, such as turning restrictions, to stop rat running and reduce through traffic on key routes like Victoria Way and Marlborough Lane
  • A new pedestrian crossing and a raised table on Victoria Way to calm traffic and improve safety; and
  • A bus gate on Eastcombe Avenue and Victoria Way (allowing buses, but not through traffic)

Since the LTNs were proposed in 2023, West Charlton Residents Association has repeatedly highlighted the concerns of Charlton residents, correctly predicting the increase in traffic on Eastcombe Avenue/Victoria Way.Pressure from WCRA helped ensure that Charlton and other boundary areas, not just the LTN area, were included for monitoring and consultation before the LTN trial began in November 2024. We believe that the LTNs should not have been made permanent in March 2026, with “success” being declared based on 1 week of data from June 2025: vehicle traffic is lower on dry summer days with long daylight hours and Silvertown Tunnel opening was likely the main reason for the drop in through traffic versus autumn 2024. We are concerned that recommendations for ongoing monitoring have been ignored since June 2025. >We also believe that the impact on the wider area of any new traffic or mitigation measures should be considered and that any mitigations should be considered alongside the School Streets for Sherington and Our Lady of Grace so that traffic and pollution problems are not just pushed elsewhere.

School Streets

A consultation by the Council that affects Sherington, Our Lady of Grace and Invicta schools. The proposals would introduce restrictions on streets around the schools, preventing access and parking at specific times during term time.

Read our response (August 2026)

The introduction of the restrictions around Sherington school will force traffic onto adjacent roads - roads which are used by children walking to school. Whilst the council recently repainted parking bay and double-yellow markings on Bramshot Avenue, they have not repainted fading markings on Eastcombe Avenue, near where children cross.

Monitoring showed traffic regularly exceeding 20mph on the upper part of Eastcombe Avenue. The WCRA have asked for a pedestrian crossing to be considered here. A quick measure would be the repainting of these faded markings around this blind bend, where near misses have been witnessed between vehicles and pedestrians (made worse when large vehicles park here).

20mph Limit Consultation

The Council is consulting on a borough-wide 20mph limit. The consultation is open until 29th September 2026. The proposal lists all the streets in our area, except:

  • Empire Close
  • Littlecombe

Note: all the streets in our area are already 20mph.
